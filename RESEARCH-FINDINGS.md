## TL;DR

The SpaceX IPO is a **genuine, in-progress event** — not a rumor. The playbook's
ticker (**SPCX**), Nasdaq venue, and June timeline check out against the primary
S-1. The two index-rule catalysts are real but **asymmetric in certainty**: the
Nasdaq-100 fast-entry rule is **adopted and live**, while the S&P 500 profitability
waiver is **only a proposal** contingent on adoption around June 8. The core
"forced index buying props up the price" thesis is **half right** — the classic
broad index pop has largely *disappeared*, but the short-seasoning froth-then-unwind
risk is real for a mega-IPO like this. The macro "strong-hands shakeout" angle is
**unverified**.

## 2. The S&P 500 "profitability waiver" & June 8 — ⚠️ REAL but a PROPOSAL

- S&P Dow Jones opened **consultation MR4292 on April 30, 2026**, proposing to exempt
  **"MegaCap" companies** (≈ ≥$112B; the 100th-largest in the S&P Total Market Index)
  from the **Financial Viability (GAAP profitability)** criteria *and* the minimum
  **0.10 IWF (float)** requirement.
- Comments **closed May 28, 2026**; possible implementation **before market open
  June 8, 2026 — IF adopted.** ⚠️ The June 8 date is **real but contingent**, not a
  confirmed inclusion event.
- ⚠️ **Media framing flag:** S&P's text says generic **"MegaCap"** and **never names
  SpaceX**. The "this is for SpaceX" angle is reporter/analyst framing. The waiver
  would apply to a *class* of giant IPOs (SpaceX, OpenAI, Anthropic all cited).
- ❌ **Refuted detail:** the widely-repeated "seasoning cut from **12 → 6 months**"
  failed verification against the primary source. Treat that specific number as
  *reported, not confirmed*.

**The existing rule SpaceX would need waived** (confirmed verbatim vs S&P methodology):
positive GAAP net income from continuing ops in the **most recent quarter** AND a
positive **sum over the four most recent quarters**.

## 3. The Nasdaq-100 "15 trading days" fast-entry rule — ✅ REAL & ADOPTED

This is the **most solid** leg of the playbook.

- **Adopted** (SEC filing SR-NASDAQ-2026-004, March 2026), **effective May 1, 2026**.
- A newcomer ranked **top-40 by market cap** can join the Nasdaq-100 **~15 trading days
  after listing** (with ≥5 days' notice, subject to a **3× float cap**) — instead of
  waiting for annual reconstitution.
- ✅ **Refinement:** the 15-day fast lane is **top-40 only**; companies **outside the
  top-40 keep the 3-month seasoning**. SpaceX would clearly qualify by size.
- ⚠️ Staleness note: some Feb-2026 sources still call this a "proposal." It's **live
  now** — the consultation closed Feb 27 and the rule was adopted.
- This is a **distinct index from the S&P 500** — it does *not* hinge on the
  profitability waiver. (The earlier confusion between the two was the right instinct
  to untangle: S&P = *eligibility/profitability*; Nasdaq-100 = *timing/seasoning*.)
- **Mechanics of the actual buy (refinement):** debut Jun 12 + ~15 trading days →
  effective **~Jul 7** (holidays Jun 19 / Jul 3 push it to the back of the Jul 2–7
  range). Index funds buy in **one closing auction the session *before* the effective
  date** (the index only holds SPCX from the effective open), not spread across the
  day — so the forced bid is a **single, float-capped print**, not a multi-day tailwind.
  Nasdaq's official notice (**≥5 trading days ahead, ~Jun 29–30**) names the exact
  effective date; the buy is then "effective − 1 session," and Phase 03 begins the next
  session.

## 4. "Forced buying props up price, then froth unwinds" — 🟡 HALF RIGHT

| Sub-claim | Finding | Verdict |
|-----------|---------|---------|
| Short-seasoning mega-IPOs get bought high, then fall | Murray & Sammon (2026): 5-day seasoning forces indexers to buy at peak; prices fall **"as much as 10%"** after | ✅ (upper bound, not typical) |
| Broad S&P "index effect" gives a reliable pop | **Disappeared.** Addition abnormal returns fell **7.4% (1990s) → ~0.3–1.0% (2010–2020)**, statistically ≈ zero | ❌ (overstated) |
| Why it faded | Trackers mechanically buy ~7–8% of shares, but **active institutions supply the liquidity**, so total institutional ownership barely moves | ✅ |

**Net:** the forced-buying *support* is **much weaker** than the strong form of the
thesis implies, but the *short-window froth-then-unwind* risk is genuine for a
fast-tracked SPCX. The playbook's Phase 02 → 03 structure (support during inclusion,
reversal risk *after* buying exhausts) is **structurally sound** — just don't expect a
large guaranteed pop, and lean on the VinFast-style low-float dynamics rather than a
broad index lift.

**Sources:** Greenwood & Sammon, *The Disappearing Index Effect* (Journal of Finance
80(2):657–698, 2025; NBER w30748); Murray & Sammon, *Primary Capital Market
Transactions and Index Funds* (SSRN 4929872, 2026); Acadian/Owenomics commentary.
